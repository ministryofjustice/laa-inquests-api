Frequently Asked Questions

What is legal aid?

Legal aid is a government-funded scheme that helps people who cannot afford legal representation to access justice. It covers various types of legal matters including civil, family, and criminal cases.

Who is eligible for legal aid?

Eligibility for legal aid depends on several factors:

  • Your income and savings
  • The type of legal problem you have
  • The merits of your case
  • Whether you qualify for any exemptions

You may need to undergo a means test to determine your financial eligibility.

How do I apply for legal aid?

To apply for legal aid, you should:

  • Contact a legal aid provider in your area
  • Complete the application form with all required details
  • Provide evidence of your income and savings
  • Submit any supporting documentation for your case
Processing times vary, but you should receive a decision within 4-6 weeks of submitting a complete application.

What types of cases does legal aid cover?

Legal aid may be available for:

  • Family matters (including domestic abuse, child protection)
  • Housing issues (such as eviction or homelessness)
  • Debt and welfare benefits
  • Immigration and asylum cases
  • Clinical negligence
  • Actions against public authorities

Not all legal matters are covered. Check with a legal aid provider to confirm if your case is eligible.

Will I have to pay anything?

This depends on your financial circumstances. You may need to pay:

  • A contribution from your income if you earn above certain thresholds
  • A contribution from any capital or savings you have
  • Costs back from any money or property you keep or gain as a result of your case

How long does legal aid last?

Legal aid certificates are granted for specific stages or aspects of your case. The duration depends on the complexity of your matter. Your legal aid provider will inform you if the certificate needs to be extended or amended.

For more information, visit www.gov.uk/legal-aid or contact the Civil Legal Advice helpline.
